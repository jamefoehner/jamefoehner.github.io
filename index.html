Activist Hedge Fund Target Screener & Post Holdings Activist Pitch

Oct 2026 · Python · SQL · WRDS (CRSP, Compustat) · SEC EDGAR · Excel

I collected 232 activist hedge fund campaigns from SEC 13D filings, measured how target stocks reacted, and trained a model to predict which companies activists will target next. I then used it to find Post Holdings and wrote a full activist pitch with an Excel valuation model.

  • +7.0%stock return vs. the market in the three days around a 13D filing (t = 13.3, 227 campaigns)
  • 27%of future targets caught in the model's top 10% in a walk-forward test, 2016–2025 (AUC 0.73)
  • 7th / 2,606Post Holdings' rank among U.S. companies; pitch: sell Weetabix, pay down debt, stop debt-funded buybacks
  • 182spin-offs since 2010 analyzed to test whether separating Post's businesses would add value

What I learned I tested two of my own assumptions against the data, that paying down debt would lift Post's valuation and that a spin-off would unlock value. Neither held, so I rebuilt the pitch around what did.

Can Language Models Read Activist Filings? An LLM Evaluation

A benchmark testing whether language models can separate real activist demands from legal boilerplate in SEC 13D filings. I hand-labeled 100 filings across ten demand categories, seat counts, proxy-fight threats and tone, writing a labeling guide as I went; a second person labeled 20 independently. With the guide, Claude Sonnet 5.5 got every demand right on 83% of filings (98% of clear ones) and matched the reference labels on all 20 shared filings, versus 18 of 20 for the second human. The written rules mattered more than model size, and filings where every model disagreed with me surfaced a 7% label error rate.

Do Activist Filings Reach Prices Slowly? An Information Diffusion Study

A follow-up to the activist project testing gradual information diffusion. Across 225 campaigns, targets absorbed only about 46% of their price move on the filing day and the next; the rest drifted in over three weeks. The drift was concentrated in stocks that normally respond slowly to market news and in filings on busy news days. The price-delay effect survived outlier trimming, median regression, an illiquidity control, and a multiple-testing adjustment. The apparent small-company effect did not, because illiquidity explained it.

Airbnb Price Prediction Model

Predicts nightly prices for more than 3,500 Boston Airbnb listings. I benchmarked five regression models with grid search and cross-validation. The tuned random forest cut average error from about $110 to $75 (32%) and raised test R² from 0.12 to 0.48. Feature importance and geospatial analysis showed location and host listing count were the strongest price drivers.
